Overview

What is a hernia?

A hernia happens when an organ or fatty tissue, usually a part of the intestine, pushes through a weak spot in the muscle wall of the abdomen. It shows up as a bulge in the groin, near the belly button or at the site of an old surgical scar. The bulge often gets bigger when you cough, strain or lift something heavy, and may disappear when you lie down.

A hernia does not heal on its own and tends to grow over time. The biggest risk is strangulation, when the trapped intestine loses its blood supply. That's a surgical emergency. This is why surgeons recommend planned repair once a hernia is diagnosed.

Veinex Health offers advanced hernia surgery in Bangalore using laparoscopic (keyhole) mesh repair. Through three tiny incisions, the surgeon places a strong mesh behind the weak area to reinforce it. Compared with open surgery, you get less pain, smaller scars, a lower infection risk and a quicker return to work.

Signs

Symptoms of hernia

A visible bulge in the groin, belly button or near a scar
The bulge grows when you cough, strain or stand
Pain or discomfort when lifting or bending
A heavy, dragging or burning sensation
Swelling in the scrotum (in some groin hernias)
Sudden severe pain, vomiting or a hard bulge (emergency)

A hernia that becomes hard, painful and won't go back in is an emergency. If you also have vomiting or can't pass gas, go to the nearest emergency or call us 24×7.

Causes

What causes hernia?

  • Heavy lifting and straining
  • Chronic cough or constipation
  • Obesity
  • Pregnancy
  • Previous abdominal surgery
  • Weak abdominal wall from birth or ageing
Types

Types of hernia we treat

The location of the hernia decides the best surgical approach.

1

Inguinal

The most common type: a bulge in the groin, much more frequent in men.

Treatment: Laparoscopic TEP or TAPP mesh repair.

2

Umbilical

A bulge at or near the belly button, common after pregnancy and in people with obesity.

Treatment: Laparoscopic or small open mesh repair.

3

Incisional

A hernia through an old surgical scar, such as after a C-section or abdominal surgery.

Treatment: Laparoscopic IPOM repair with mesh.

4

Ventral / epigastric

A bulge in the upper or middle abdominal wall.

Treatment: Laparoscopic mesh repair.

Keyhole repair of groin hernias with mesh placed behind the muscle. Both sides can be repaired in one sitting.

Laparoscopic IPOM

For umbilical, ventral and incisional hernias. A special mesh reinforces the abdominal wall from inside.

Open mesh repair (Lichtenstein)

A reliable option for selected patients, such as those who can't have general anaesthesia.

Emergency hernia surgery

24×7 care for obstructed or strangulated hernias.
