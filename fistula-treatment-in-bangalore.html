Overview

What is an anal fistula?

An anal fistula (fistula-in-ano) is a small tunnel that forms between the inside of the anal canal and the skin near the anus. It usually starts as an anal abscess, a painful collection of pus from an infected gland. When the abscess bursts or is drained, it can leave behind a tract that keeps discharging pus or blood.

A fistula does not heal with antibiotics or dressings alone. The tract stays open and keeps getting reinfected, which is why many patients in Bangalore come to us after months of recurring boils and discharge. Some fistulas pass through the sphincter muscle that controls bowel movements, so the choice of surgery matters.

Veinex Health offers advanced fistula treatment in Bangalore using fistula laser closure (FiLaC) and video-assisted anal fistula treatment (VAAFT). These techniques close the tract from within while protecting the sphincter muscle, so continence is preserved and recovery is faster.

Signs

Symptoms of anal fistula

Pus, blood or foul-smelling discharge near the anus
A small opening in the skin around the anus
Pain and swelling that gets worse while sitting
A boil near the anus that keeps coming back
Irritation and itching of the surrounding skin
Fever and tiredness when it's infected

Don't ignore a recurring boil near the anus. Untreated fistulas can branch into complex tracts that are harder to treat. An MRI fistulogram helps us map the tract precisely.

Causes

What causes fistula-in-Ano?

  • A previous anal abscess
  • Infection of the anal glands
  • Crohn's disease or other inflammatory bowel disease
  • Tuberculosis (TB) in some cases
  • Injury or earlier anal surgery
  • Diabetes, which increases infection risk
Types

Types of anal fistula

Fistulas are classified by how the tract passes through the sphincter muscles. The type decides the safest treatment.

1

Simple (low)

The tract passes through only a small part of the sphincter muscle, or below it.

Treatment: Laser closure or fistulotomy, with excellent results.

2

Trans-sphincteric

The tract crosses both the internal and external sphincter muscles.

Treatment: Sphincter-saving laser (FiLaC) or VAAFT is preferred.

3

Complex / high

Branching tracts, multiple openings or a tract high above the sphincter. Often recurrent.

Treatment: MRI mapping, then VAAFT, laser or staged seton treatment.

Treatment

Fistula treatment options at Veinex Health, Bangalore

Your surgeon chooses the technique after mapping the tract, usually with an MRI fistulogram.

Fistula laser closure (FiLaC)

A radial laser fibre is passed through the tract and seals it from within, with no large wound and the sphincter muscle kept intact.

VAAFT

A tiny camera lets the surgeon see inside the tract, find hidden branches, clean them and close the internal opening.

Seton placement

For complex fistulas, a soft thread drains infection and prepares the tract for definitive treatment.

Fistulotomy

For simple, low fistulas, the tract is laid open so it heals from the base up.
