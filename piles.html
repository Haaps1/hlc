Overview

What are piles (hemorrhoids)?

Piles are swollen, inflamed veins in and around the anus and lower rectum. They're very common: about half of all adults experience them by age 50. They can cause bleeding, itching, pain and a lump near the anus.

Mild piles can often be managed with diet and medicines. Persistent, bleeding or prolapsed piles usually need treatment, and laser hemorrhoidoplasty is today's most comfortable option.

Symptoms

Signs you may have piles

Bright red bleeding during or after bowel movements
Itching or irritation around the anus
A lump or swelling near the anus
Pain or discomfort while sitting
Feeling of incomplete bowel evacuation
Mucus discharge after passing stool

Don't self-diagnose rectal bleeding. It can also be caused by fissures, polyps or other conditions. A quick, private examination with our specialist will confirm the cause.

Stages

The 4 grades of internal piles

Tap each grade to see what it means and how it's treated.

1

Grade 1

Piles stay inside the anal canal. You may notice painless bleeding during bowel movements.

Treatment: Diet changes, medicines or a quick laser procedure.

2

Grade 2

Piles come out while passing stool but go back inside on their own. Itching and discomfort are common.

Treatment: Laser hemorrhoidoplasty is highly effective at this stage.

3

Grade 3

Piles come out and must be pushed back in by hand. Bleeding, pain and mucus discharge increase.

Treatment: Laser or stapler procedure is usually recommended.

4

Grade 4

Piles stay outside and cannot be pushed back. They can become thrombosed and very painful.

Treatment: Needs surgical treatment soon. Laser is still an option in many cases.

Causes

What causes piles?

  • Chronic constipation and straining
  • Low-fibre diet and low water intake
  • Sitting for long hours
  • Pregnancy and childbirth
  • Obesity and lack of exercise
  • Heavy lifting and ageing

Procedure

How laser piles treatment works

Consultation & diagnosis

A private examination (with proctoscopy if needed) to confirm the grade and plan treatment.

Pre-procedure tests

Basic blood tests and fitness check, done on the same day at our centre.

Anaesthesia

Local or spinal anaesthesia, so you feel no pain during the procedure.

Laser hemorrhoidoplasty

A thin laser fibre shrinks the piles mass from within by sealing its blood supply. No cuts, no stitches.

Recovery & discharge

Rest for a few hours, then go home the same day with medicines and a diet plan.

FAQs

Piles treatment FAQs

The procedure is done under anaesthesia so it's painless. Afterwards, most patients have only mild discomfort for a day or two, managed with oral painkillers.

Cost depends on the grade of piles, anaesthesia type and room choice. You'll receive a transparent, all-inclusive estimate after your free consultation, and it's usually covered by insurance.

Recurrence after laser treatment is very low. Following the recommended high-fibre diet, drinking enough water and avoiding straining keeps it that way.

Early grade 1 piles often improve with diet, lifestyle changes and medicines. Grade 2 and above, or piles that keep bleeding, usually need a procedure.

You can eat a light meal a few hours after the procedure. Your dietitian will share a simple high-fibre diet plan to follow for the next few weeks.
