Overview

What is a pilonidal sinus?

A pilonidal sinus is a small tunnel or pit in the skin at the top of the buttock cleft, just above the tailbone. Loose hairs get pushed into the skin, the body treats them as foreign material, and a painful infected cyst or abscess forms. It often discharges pus or blood and tends to keep coming back.

Pilonidal sinus mostly affects young adults, especially men with thick body hair and people who sit for long hours. That's why we see many cases among Bangalore's software professionals, students and cab and delivery drivers.

Traditional open surgery leaves a large wound that needs weeks of daily dressings. At Veinex Health we offer minimally invasive pilonidal sinus treatment in Bangalore, including laser sinus closure, which cleans and seals the tract through tiny openings for faster healing and less time off work.

Signs

Symptoms of pilonidal sinus

A painful lump or swelling near the tailbone
Pus or blood draining from a small opening
Foul smell from the discharge
Pain that gets worse when sitting
Fever when the sinus is infected
Abscesses that keep coming back in the same spot

An acute pilonidal abscess needs prompt drainage. Leaving it untreated can lead to spreading infection and more complex sinus tracts.

Causes

What causes pilonidal Sinus?

  • Loose hair penetrating the skin
  • Sitting for long hours (desk jobs, driving)
  • Thick or coarse body hair
  • Deep buttock cleft
  • Excess sweating and friction
  • Obesity
Treatment

Pilonidal sinus treatment options at Veinex Health, Bangalore

We choose the least invasive option that suits the size and complexity of your sinus.

Laser sinus closure (SiLaC)

Hairs and debris are removed and a laser fibre seals the tract from within. Tiny wounds and faster healing.

Endoscopic treatment (EPSiT)

A small camera guides complete cleaning of the sinus and its branches through a tiny opening.

Pit picking

For small, early sinuses, the pits are removed with minimal cuts.

Flap reconstruction

For large or repeatedly recurrent sinuses, the area is removed and closed with a flap (such as Limberg) to flatten the cleft.

FAQs

Pilonidal Sinus Treatment in Bangalore: FAQs

For most patients, minimally invasive laser sinus closure is the preferred option because it leaves tiny wounds, needs fewer dressings and allows a faster return to work. Large or repeatedly recurrent sinuses may need a flap procedure.

Antibiotics and drainage can settle an infection, but the sinus tract usually remains and the problem returns. A procedure is needed for lasting relief.

The procedure is done under anaesthesia, and most people have only mild discomfort afterwards, far less than after open excision.

Recurrence is low when the tract is completely cleared. Keeping the area clean and removing hair (for example with laser hair reduction) further lowers the risk.

It depends on the size of the sinus, the technique and whether an abscess needs treating first. You'll get a clear estimate after your consultation, and the surgery is usually covered by insurance.

After laser treatment most people return to desk work in 3–5 days. Avoid long periods of sitting on hard surfaces and heavy exercise for 2–3 weeks.

It's most common in young men between 15 and 40, people with thick body hair, and those who sit for long hours, such as IT professionals and drivers.
