Overview

What is an anal fissure?

An anal fissure is a small tear in the thin lining of the anal canal. It usually happens when you pass a hard or large stool, and it causes a sharp, cutting pain during bowel movements followed by a burning ache that can last for hours. Many people mistake a fissure for piles, but the treatment is different.

The pain makes the anal sphincter muscle go into spasm. That spasm reduces blood flow to the tear, which stops it from healing, and a vicious cycle begins. A fissure that lasts longer than 6 weeks is called a chronic fissure, and it often needs a procedure to break this cycle.

At Veinex Health, our proctologists offer complete fissure treatment in Bangalore, from medicines and diet correction for new fissures to laser sphincterotomy for chronic ones. The goal is simple: stop the pain, heal the tear and protect your sphincter function.

Signs

Symptoms of anal fissure

Sharp, cutting pain while passing stool
Burning or throbbing pain for hours afterwards
Streaks of bright red blood on stool or tissue
A small skin tag (sentinel pile) near the anus
Itching or irritation around the anus
Fear of passing stool, leading to more constipation

A fissure that doesn't heal in 6 weeks needs a specialist. Repeated tearing can create a chronic wound, a skin tag and, rarely, an infection or fistula.

Causes

What causes anal Fissure?

  • Passing hard or large stools
  • Chronic constipation and straining
  • Frequent diarrhoea
  • Low-fibre diet and dehydration
  • Childbirth
  • Tight or overactive anal sphincter muscle
Types

Acute vs chronic fissure

How long you've had the fissure changes the treatment plan.

1

Acute

A fresh tear, less than 6 weeks old. The edges are clean and it often heals with the right care.

Treatment: Stool softeners, fibre, sitz baths and medicated ointments that relax the sphincter.

2

Chronic

Present for more than 6 weeks, with raised edges, a skin tag or visible muscle fibres at the base.

Treatment: Laser sphincterotomy or fissurectomy for lasting healing.

3

Recurrent

A fissure that heals and then keeps coming back, often due to a tight sphincter.

Treatment: Laser treatment to relieve the spasm and correct the cause.
