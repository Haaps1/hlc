Overview

What are varicose veins?

Varicose veins are swollen, twisted veins that bulge under the skin, most often on the legs. Veins carry blood back to the heart using tiny one-way valves. When these valves weaken, blood flows backwards and pools in the leg veins, which stretch, bulge and become painful. Doctors call this chronic venous insufficiency.

Varicose veins are far more than a cosmetic problem. Without treatment they can cause heaviness, swelling, night cramps, skin darkening, eczema and eventually non-healing venous ulcers near the ankle. In Bangalore we see them often in people whose jobs keep them on their feet for hours, such as teachers, nurses, traffic police, retail and security staff, as well as in women after pregnancy.

Signs

Symptoms of varicose veins

Bulging, twisted, blue or purple veins on the legs
Heavy, tired or aching legs by evening
Swelling around the ankles and feet
Night cramps and restless legs
Itching or burning over the veins
Skin darkening, hardening or ulcers near the ankle

Seek care quickly if a vein bleeds, becomes red, hot and painful, or if a leg suddenly swells. These can signal bleeding, superficial thrombophlebitis or a deep vein clot.

Causes

What causes varicose Veins?

  • Standing or sitting for long hours at work
  • Family history of varicose veins
  • Pregnancy and hormonal changes
  • Increasing age
  • Obesity
  • Previous deep vein thrombosis (DVT)
Types

Stages of venous disease (CEAP)

Vein specialists grade venous disease from C1 to C6. Earlier treatment is simpler and prevents complications.

C1–C2

C1–C2

Spider veins (C1) and visible bulging varicose veins (C2), with heaviness or aching.

Treatment: Doppler scan, then laser/RFA or sclerotherapy.

C3

C3

Ankle and leg swelling (oedema) along with varicose veins.

Treatment: Endovenous ablation and compression to relieve swelling.

C4

C4

Skin changes: darkening, eczema or hardened skin around the ankle.

Treatment: Prompt ablation to stop skin damage progressing.

C5–C6

C5–C6

Healed (C5) or active (C6) venous ulcer that is slow to heal.

Treatment: Ablation plus specialised ulcer and compression care.

Treatment

Varicose veins treatment options at Veinex Health, Bangalore

Your vein specialist chooses the treatment after a colour venous Doppler scan maps the faulty veins.

Endovenous laser ablation (EVLA)

A thin laser fibre is guided into the faulty vein under ultrasound and seals it from inside. Blood reroutes through healthy veins.

Radiofrequency ablation (RFA)

Uses controlled heat from radiofrequency energy to close the diseased vein, with a similar quick recovery.

VenaSeal (medical glue)

A medical-grade adhesive closes the vein with no heat and often no need for compression stockings afterwards.

Foam sclerotherapy

An injection that collapses smaller varicose and spider veins, often combined with ablation.
