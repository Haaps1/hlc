Overview

What is diabetic foot?

"Diabetic foot" describes foot problems caused by long-term diabetes. High blood sugar damages the nerves (neuropathy), so you may not feel a blister, cut or pressure sore. It also narrows the blood vessels (peripheral artery disease), so wounds get less oxygen and heal slowly. Together, these turn a small cut into a serious ulcer or infection.

India has one of the largest populations of people living with diabetes in the world, and diabetic foot ulcers are a leading cause of hospital admissions and amputations. The good news is that most of these amputations can be prevented with early, expert care.

Signs

Warning signs of diabetic foot

A cut, blister or wound that isn't healing
Numbness, tingling or burning in the feet
Change in skin colour: red, dark or black patches
Discharge, pus or bad smell from a wound
Swelling, warmth or change in foot shape
Cold feet or pain in the calves while walking

Black skin, spreading redness, fever or a foul-smelling wound is an emergency. Infection in a diabetic foot can spread quickly. Please come in the same day.

Causes

What causes diabetic Foot?

  • Poorly controlled blood sugar
  • Nerve damage (diabetic neuropathy)
  • Reduced blood flow (peripheral artery disease)
  • Ill-fitting footwear and walking barefoot
  • Smoking
  • Corns, calluses and foot deformities
Types

Grades of diabetic foot ulcer (Wagner)

Doctors grade diabetic foot ulcers to plan treatment. Early grades heal far more easily.

0–1

Grade 0–1

At-risk foot or a superficial ulcer involving only the skin.

Treatment: Wound care, offloading and footwear advice.

2

Grade 2

A deeper ulcer reaching tendon or joint capsule, without abscess.

Treatment: Debridement, advanced dressings and offloading.

3

Grade 3

Deep ulcer with abscess or bone infection (osteomyelitis).

Treatment: Surgical drainage, antibiotics and specialist care.

4–5

Grade 4–5

Gangrene of part or all of the foot.

Treatment: Urgent surgery and revascularisation to save the limb.

Treatment

Diabetic foot treatment at Veinex Health, Bangalore

Healing a diabetic foot needs several things at once. We coordinate all of them for you.

Wound debridement

Dead and infected tissue is carefully removed so healthy tissue can grow and dressings can work.

Advanced dressings & NPWT

Modern dressings and negative-pressure wound therapy (vacuum dressing) speed up healing of deep ulcers.

Offloading

Special footwear, casts or insoles take pressure off the ulcer so it can close.

Circulation care

Doppler assessment and, where needed, referral for angioplasty to restore blood flow to the foot.
